MOTs and annual tests

Vans take the standard MOT from three years old. Lorries over 3,500kg, and many trailers, take an annual test from their first year. Here's what applies to what.

Great BritainLast checked 22 October 2026

Vans and cars

  • The first MOT is due by the third anniversary of registration, then every year.
  • You can get an MOT up to a month (minus a day) before it runs out and keep the same renewal date.
  • Goods vehicles up to 3,000kg design gross weight take a Class 4 MOT. Those over 3,000kg and up to 3,500kg take a Class 7 MOT.
  • Driving without a valid MOT can mean a fine of up to £1,000.

Lorries and trailers (the annual test)

  • Goods vehicles with a gross weight over 3,500kg need an annual test. The first is due 12 months after first registration.
  • Trailers that need the test include those with an unladen weight over 1,020kg and powered braking, semi-trailers, and A-frame trailers and converter dollies made on or after 1 January 1979. A trailer's first test is usually due one year after it was first sold or supplied.
  • Some vehicles are exempt from the annual test; form V112/G lists them. An exempt vehicle might still need a standard MOT.
Changed on 1 June 2026: electric vans up to 4,250kg

Zero-emission goods vehicles with a design gross weight between 3,501kg and 4,250kg now take a Class 7 MOT instead of the annual test for heavy vehicles. More on electric vans

Good practice

  • Booking a safety inspection shortly before the test gives the vehicle the best chance of passing first time.
  • Keep test dates in the same planner as your safety inspections, so nothing runs out unnoticed.
