Tyres and speed limiters

Tyres are among the most common roadside defects, and the rules change with vehicle weight. Speed limiters are a safety inspection item on heavier vehicles.

Tread depth

VehicleLegal minimum
Cars, vans up to 3,500kg, and trailers up to 3,500kg1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tread, around the entire circumference
Vehicles over 3,500kg (lorries, buses)1mm across at least three-quarters of the tread's width, around the entire circumference. The base of the original tread grooves must also be clearly visible.

Zero-emission vans between 3,500kg and 4,250kg follow the 1.6mm rule for lighter vehicles. More on electric vans

Tyre age

  • Since 1 February 2021, tyres over 10 years old can't be used on the front steered axles of lorries, buses and coaches, or on any single wheel of a minibus (9 to 16 passenger seats).
  • The manufacturer's date code must be legible on all tyres on lorries, buses, coaches, minibuses and trailers over 3.5 tonnes.
  • At a roadside check, a tyre over 10 years old on a lorry's front steered axle gets an immediate prohibition. At the annual test, it's a dangerous fail.
  • Where tyres over 10 years old are still legally used (for example on rear axles), DVSA expects their age to be recorded and a risk assessment done for the distance, speed and loads involved.
Reading a date code

The date code is four digits: the week, then the year. 3307 means week 33 of 2007. Retreaded tyres can carry two date codes, and the more recent one is used to judge the tyre's age.

At every safety inspection

  • Record tread depths, and tyre pressures as presented. Investigate any under-inflated tyre and record what was done.
  • Flag a tyre as an advisory when it reaches 9 years old, and report it as a defect once it's over 10 years old and can't legally be used.
  • Report illegible date codes and unserviceable tyres as defects, with the action taken.
After a wheel is refitted

The industry code of practice says to re-check wheel nut torque after the vehicle has stood for 30 minutes, or after 40 to 80km (25 to 50 miles). DVSA says every re-torque check must be recorded and kept on file.

Speed limiters

  • Goods vehicles over 3,500kg first used on or after 1 January 2005 must have a speed limiter that stops them exceeding 90km/h (56mph). Some older goods vehicles are covered too, depending on their weight, engine and when they were first used.
  • The speed limiter is an item on DVSA's example safety inspection record for lorries, so it should be checked at every inspection.
