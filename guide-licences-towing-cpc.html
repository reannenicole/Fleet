Driving licences, towing and Driver CPC

From Fleet Compliance Desk · Great Britain · Last checked 22 October 2026

Licence categories are based on maximum authorised mass (MAM): the most a vehicle, or a vehicle and trailer, can weigh when fully loaded. A van that's fine on a car licence can need a bigger entitlement once you add a trailer.

Car licence (category B)

  • Covers vehicles with a MAM up to 3,500kg and no more than 8 passenger seats.
  • On category B alone, you can tow a trailer up to 750kg MAM, or a heavier trailer as long as the vehicle and trailer together are no more than 3,500kg.
  • A full car licence also covers zero-emission vans up to 4,250kg MAM. They can tow as long as the combined MAM is no more than 7,000kg and the trailer's MAM is no more than 3,500kg.

Towing bigger trailers (category BE)

  • Since 16 December 2021, car drivers can tow trailers up to 3,500kg MAM (category BE) without taking an extra test.
  • If you passed your car test on or after 1 January 1997, DVLA adds category BE the next time you renew your licence.

Bigger vans and light trucks (C1)

  • C1 covers vehicles between 3,500kg and 7,500kg, with a trailer up to 750kg.
  • C1E covers the same vehicles with a heavier trailer, up to a combined weight of 12,000kg.
  • Drivers who passed their car test before 1 January 1997 can usually drive a vehicle and trailer combination up to 8,250kg MAM (C1E, restriction code 107) until their licence expires at 70.

Driver CPC

  • Professional lorry, bus and coach drivers need 35 hours of periodic training every 5 years. One set of training covers both lorries and buses.
  • To drive professionally in the UK and Europe, all 35 hours must be International courses. If any of the hours are National courses, you get a National Driver CPC and can only drive professionally in the UK.
  • Driving professionally without a valid Driver CPC can mean a fine of up to £1,000.
Good practice

Check driving licences when someone joins and at regular intervals after that. Drivers can share their licence details with an employer using a check code from GOV.UK's view or share your driving licence information (opens in a new tab) service.
