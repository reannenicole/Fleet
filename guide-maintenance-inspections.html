Maintenance and safety inspections

From Fleet Compliance Desk · Great Britain · Last checked 22 October 2026

Vehicles must be roadworthy every time they're used, not just on test day. DVSA's Guide to maintaining roadworthiness sets out what a good maintenance system looks like, and it's the standard operators are judged against.

The key rules

  • A walkaround check must be done at least once every day (or 24-hour period) the vehicle is used, preferably just before it's used. If someone other than the driver does it, the driver needs to see evidence it's been done.
  • Any defect that makes a vehicle unroadworthy must be fixed before the vehicle is used.
  • Safety inspections must happen at the frequency recorded on VOL, and cover at least everything in the statutory annual test.
  • Every safety inspection should include a brake performance assessment, using a roller brake test (RBT), an electronic brake performance monitoring system (EBPMS), or a decelerometer with brake temperature readings. A laden roller brake test is the most effective method, and it can be done up to 14 days before the inspection.
  • Safety inspection records and driver defect reports must be kept for at least 15 months, including for vehicles you've since sold or taken off the licence.
  • A newly acquired, leased, hired or borrowed vehicle needs a first use inspection before it goes on the road, covering at least the annual test items. It isn't needed if there's enough evidence the vehicle has just had a safety inspection.
  • You're responsible for vehicles maintained for you by contractors or hire companies. If you contract out safety inspections, you need a written agreement.
  • Whoever uses a towing vehicle is responsible for the roadworthiness of its trailer, even if the trailer belongs to someone else.

How often to inspect

DVSA says safety inspection intervals normally range from 4 to 13 weeks, depending on how hard the vehicle works:

Operating conditionsTypical interval
Lightly loaded, easy conditions10 to 13 weeks
General haulage, trunking6 to 10 weeks
Arduous work, constant heavy loads4 to 6 weeks
Off-road, difficult conditions4 weeks
Vehicle or trailer 12 years or older6 weeks (may be extended, depending on use)

Review the interval against what inspections actually find, and update VOL if it changes. Vehicles that have been off the road for a while, or are used only part of the year, must be inspected before they go back into service.

Vans and other vehicles outside operator licensing

Light goods vehicles that need an operator's licence must be maintained in line with DVSA's guide. DVSA also expects licence holders to have a suitable maintenance regime for vehicles that are outside operator licensing, such as light vans, and says the Traffic Commissioner may take a failure to maintain them into account.

Good practice

  • Plan inspection dates at least six months ahead using a planner or maintenance system.
  • Have a clear system for taking unroadworthy vehicles off the road, run by someone with the authority to do it.
  • The transport manager or another responsible person should review each completed inspection record before the vehicle goes back into service.
  • Include a safety recall check at every inspection. DVSA's example inspection records for lorries and vans both have a line for it.
  • An inspection isn't a service. Servicing follows the manufacturer's schedule; inspections check roadworthiness. You need both.
