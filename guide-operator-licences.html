Operator licences (O-licences)

From Fleet Compliance Desk · Great Britain · Last checked 22 October 2026

If a business uses goods vehicles over 3,500kg, it normally needs a goods vehicle operator's licence from the Traffic Commissioners. The licence comes with promises about how vehicles are maintained, and the Traffic Commissioners can act if those promises aren't kept.

When you need one

  • You need a licence to carry goods in a vehicle with a gross plated weight over 3,500kg, or an unladen weight over 1,525kg if it has no plated weight.
  • With a trailer, you need one if the plated weights add up to more than 3,500kg. You don't need one if the trailer weighs under 1,020kg unladen and you only carry your own goods.
  • Since May 2022, vans over 2,500kg (or a van or car towing a trailer with a gross train weight over 2,500kg) carrying other people's goods for hire or reward in the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway or Switzerland need a standard international licence.

The three types

LicenceWhat you can carry
RestrictedYour own goods only ("own account")
Standard nationalYour own goods, plus other people's goods for hire or reward in the UK
Standard internationalYour own goods, plus other people's goods for hire or reward in the UK and abroad
Watch out

"Own account" and "hire or reward" are different. Carrying other people's goods for payment needs a standard licence, not a restricted one.

What the licence commits you to

  • When you apply, you give undertakings (once called statements of intent), including that vehicles will have regular safety checks and inspections at set intervals, with records kept.
  • Your inspection frequency is recorded on the Vehicle Operator Licensing (VOL) system. Inspections must happen at that frequency, and any change to your maintenance arrangements must be updated on VOL.
  • You need a separate licence for each Traffic Area where you have an operating centre. One licence covers any number of operating centres in the same Traffic Area.
  • DVSA sends a copy of every prohibition to the Traffic Commissioner. Traffic Commissioners can curtail, suspend or revoke a licence because of prohibitions, convictions, or a failure to meet its conditions, including keeping vehicles properly maintained.

Your compliance record

  • DVSA gives every operator an Operator Compliance Risk Score (OCRS), based on results from annual tests and roadside checks. The higher the risk, the more likely your vehicles are to be stopped.
  • Operators with audited compliance systems can join DVSA Earned Recognition. Members get the lowest risk status, and their vehicles are less likely to be stopped at the roadside.

Good practice

  • Keep the vehicle list and inspection frequency on VOL accurate, because that's what your records are checked against.
  • Check which licence rules apply before a heavier van, or anything that tows, goes into service.
