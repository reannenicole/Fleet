Drivers' hours and tachographs

From Fleet Compliance Desk · Great Britain · Last checked 22 October 2026

Most goods vehicles over 3.5 tonnes follow the assimilated rules (the former EU rules, kept in UK law) and need a tachograph. Vans used only in Great Britain usually follow the simpler GB domestic rules.

Which rules apply

  • Assimilated rules apply to most vehicles carrying goods where the maximum permissible mass, including any trailer, is over 3.5 tonnes.
  • From 1 July 2026, they also apply to light goods vehicles over 2.5 tonnes on international journeys. There's an exemption for own-account work (not for hire or reward) where driving isn't the driver's main activity.
  • GB domestic rules apply to goods vehicles that don't fall under the assimilated rules, which covers most vans used only in Great Britain.
  • Zero-emission goods vehicles between 3.5 and 4.25 tonnes are exempt from the assimilated rules and tachographs in Great Britain, with no limit on distance. The exemption doesn't apply in Northern Ireland.

Assimilated rules: the limits

RuleLimit
Daily driving9 hours, which can be extended to 10 hours twice a week
Weekly driving56 hours
Two-week driving90 hours in any two consecutive weeks
Breaks45 minutes after no more than 4.5 hours of driving, or split into 15 minutes then 30 minutes
Daily rest11 hours. Can be reduced to 9 hours no more than 3 times between weekly rests, or split into 3 hours then 9 hours (12 hours in total)
Weekly rest45 hours, or a reduced 24 hours that must be compensated. Must start within six 24-hour periods of the end of the last weekly rest.

GB domestic rules: the limits

  • No more than 10 hours of driving in a day.
  • No more than 11 hours on duty in a working day. The duty limit doesn't apply on days you don't drive, and if you don't drive for more than 4 hours on any day of the week, it doesn't apply for that whole week.
  • Drivers of vans up to 3.5 tonnes used for inspection, cleaning, maintenance, repair, installation or fitting work (and a few other uses) are exempt from the duty limit, but not the 10-hour driving limit.
  • Drivers of goods vehicles that need an operator's licence generally keep a weekly record sheet, or can use a tachograph instead. Some drivers are exempt from keeping records, so check the guidance.

Tachographs

  • A tachograph must be used when driving under the assimilated or AETR rules.
  • Download driver cards at least every 28 days and vehicle units at least every 90 days.
  • Drivers must be able to show their records at the roadside for the current day and the previous 28 days (56 days for international journeys to and from the EU).
  • Operators must keep records for 12 months and be able to produce them to enforcement officers.
  • From 24 December 2025, newly registered goods vehicles that need a tachograph must have a full smart 2 tachograph. From 1 July 2026, goods vehicles over 2.5 tonnes used for international hire or reward journeys need one too.
  • Goods vehicles over 2.5 tonnes and under 3.5 tonnes used only in the UK don't need a tachograph.

Operator responsibilities

  • Check tachograph records and data regularly, and act on what you find.
  • Make sure drivers are trained and instructed on the rules.
  • Plan work so drivers can stay within the rules, and don't pay in ways that encourage breaking them.
