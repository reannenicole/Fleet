Vehicle safety recalls

Manufacturers recall vehicles, parts and accessories when they find a safety defect. Fleet vehicles are easy to miss, so it pays to check rather than wait for a letter.

How recalls reach you

  • Manufacturers can ask DVLA to find the registered keepers of affected vehicles, so keep the keeper details on each vehicle's log book (V5C) up to date.
  • Keeper records aren't always available, for example for trailers and some components. Manufacturers have to reach owners in other ways, so don't rely on getting a letter.

Recall types

TypeWhat it means
Safety recall (stop drive)An immediate threat to safety. The vehicle must not be driven.
Safety recallA safety defect, but the threat isn't immediate or can be reduced by the owner taking reasonable steps. Get it fixed.
Consumer or garage warningA safety defect that can be managed through maintenance or similar checks. Sometimes issued alongside a recall.

Manufacturers also run non-safety recalls and service campaigns for problems that don't affect safety.

Good practice

  • Check every vehicle with the GOV.UK recall checker before it goes into service, and again at safety inspections. DVSA's example inspection records include a safety recall check.
  • Keep a simple tracker for open recalls, so every affected vehicle is followed through to completion.
